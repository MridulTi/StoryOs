# Product Philosophy

Decisions about what StoryOS is and is not — sourced from the project vision.

## Discovery over generation

**Status:** active  
**Evidence:** confirmed  
**Source:** [foundation.md](../foundation.md) — Philosophy, Core Principle, Guiding Principles  
**Revisit when:** product scope expands into new AI capabilities

StoryOS discovers stories already present in the creator's life. It does not generate fiction, invent experiences, or produce content from topic prompts.

**Reason:** Creators don't lack writing ability — they lack recognition of their own material. The core loop is Life → Capture → Understand → Find Story, not Topic → Prompt → Generate.

**Rejected alternative:** Build as an AI writing/script tool (ChatGPT-style "write me a post about X"). Rejected because it assumes the user already knows what to write, misses the memory problem, and replaces the creator as author — violating authenticity.

## Creator is always the author

**Status:** active  
**Evidence:** confirmed  
**Source:** [foundation.md](../foundation.md) — Philosophy, Story Companion, Guiding Principles  

AI roles are bounded to: Observer, Memory, Interviewer, Story Coach. AI is never the storyteller or protagonist.

**Reason:** Authenticity is the product's core value. If audiences sense AI authorship, trust collapses and the product becomes interchangeable with generic generators.

**Rejected alternative:** AI drafts full narrative prose for one-click publish. Rejected — Story Companion interviews instead; multiplication reframes creator words, not substitutes them.

## "What happened today?" not "What do you want to write?"

**Status:** active  
**Evidence:** confirmed  
**Source:** [foundation.md](../foundation.md) — Product Vision  

The primary entry question orientates around experience, not intent to produce content.

**Reason:** Shifts mental model from performance ("I must create") to reflection ("I already lived something"). Reduces blank-page anxiety.

## Every memory is valuable

**Status:** active  
**Evidence:** confirmed  
**Source:** [foundation.md](../foundation.md) — Guiding Principles  

Low story scores do not mean discard. Private journal moments matter even when relatability is low.

**Reason:** Success is "too many stories to choose from," not "only high-scoring content gets saved."

## Success metric

**Status:** active  
**Evidence:** confirmed  
**Source:** [foundation.md](../foundation.md) — Success Metric  

User stops saying "I don't know what to post" and starts saying "I have too many meaningful stories to choose from."

Use this to evaluate feature proposals: does it improve discovery and choice, or just speed up generic output?
